How to read this page

This explains what every number and badge on the watchlist means, and exactly how it's calculated — pulled straight from this page's own code, not guesswork.
This is not investment advice. Everything below is a mechanical calculation from price/volume history. None of it knows why a stock moved, what's happening with the company, or your personal risk tolerance. Treat every badge as a prompt to go research further, not as a buy/sell instruction.

The top-line signal badge

Each card shows one big pill — Entry+, Entry, Watch, Exit, or Exit+. It's a single combined score built from all the smaller indicator badges below it, so you don't have to mentally combine eight numbers yourself.

BadgeMeaning
Entry+Strongest buy-side setup — most indicators agree, and the trend is strong.
EntryBuy-side setup — indicators lean bullish/oversold.
WatchMixed signals — no strong lean either way.
ExitSell-side setup — indicators lean bearish/overbought (for a position you already hold).
Exit+Strongest sell-side setup.

How the score is built — each factor below adds or subtracts points, then the total maps to one of the five tiers above:

FactorVote
Price below a recent low (7d/15d/30d/90d/180d/365d)+1 to +3 (deeper/longer low = bigger vote)
Price above a recent high−1 to −3 (same scale, in reverse)
RSI ≤ 30 (oversold) / ≥ 70 (overbought)+1 / −1
Stochastic oversold / overbought+1 / −1
Price below lower Bollinger Band / above upper band+1 / −1
Trend + MACD + Supertrend direction (combined into one vote, see note)+1 / −1, ×2 if ADX shows a strong trend, ×0 if ADX shows a weak/no trend
Trend, MACD, and Supertrend are three different ways of asking the same question — "is this stock trending up or down?" — so they're bundled into a single vote instead of three, otherwise they'd just outvote the genuinely different indicators (RSI, Stochastic, Bollinger Bands). ADX then scales that one combined vote up when the trend is strong, or zeroes it out when there's no real trend to confirm.

Below the signal badge

Vol confirmed

Only appears on Entry/Entry+ cards. It means trading volume was at least 1.2× the normal average on this signal. In an informal backtest on the core watchlist, Entry signals with confirmed volume won noticeably more often (67% vs 55%) than those without — so it's a visual nudge that this particular Entry looks more trustworthy, not a separate scored factor.

Buy / Stop and Sell / Trail stop lines

On an Entry/Entry+ card: Buy ~$X is just the current price, and Stop ~$Y is price minus 2× ATR (Average True Range) — a volatility-scaled reference for where a stop-loss might sit. It's shown for reference only; the page doesn't enforce it as a rule (testing showed a hard stop at that exact distance actually hurt returns — the Exit signal is meant to do that job instead).

On an Exit/Exit+ card: Sell ~$X is the current price, and Trail stop ~$Y is the current Supertrend value — the same number shown in the Supertrend badge.

Sticker price row (fundamentals)

This only appears after you click Update Fundamentals on the main page (it's not fetched automatically, since fundamentals change quarterly, not daily). It's a rough intrinsic-value estimate, in the style of Phil Town's "Rule #1" sticker price method:

FieldMeaning
Sticker $Estimated fair-value price, projected from the growth rate shown
MOS $ (Margin of Safety)A discounted price below the sticker price — buying at or below this leaves room for estimation error
Growth %The assumed future growth rate used to project the sticker price
Bargain / Fair value / Above stickerWhere the current price sits relative to those two lines
Change the growth-rate assumption and the sticker price moves a lot — a "Bargain" label is only as good as that assumption. Worth sanity-checking against your own view of the company, not taking at face value.

The colored left border & corner badge

Separate from the signal badge — this just flags when price has broken a recent low or high outright:

  • Amber→red (below 7d up to below 365d low) = price fell under a recent floor. Darker/redder = a longer, more significant floor broken.
  • Light→dark blue (above 7d up to above 365d high) = price broke out above a recent ceiling.
  • "No new low/high" = price is within its recent range, nothing broken either way.

This is the same legend shown at the top of the main watchlist page.

The 7d/15d/30d/90d/180d/365d grid

For each lookback window, L is the lowest close and H is the highest close in that period. A bolded/colored L means price is currently below that window's low; a bolded/colored H means it's currently above that window's high. Use it to see at a glance how stretched the current price is versus its own recent history.

News

Chart (top tickers only)
