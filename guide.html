How to read this page

This explains what every number and badge on the watchlist means, and exactly how it's calculated — pulled straight from this page's own code, not guesswork.
This is not investment advice. Everything below is a mechanical calculation from price/volume history. None of it knows why a stock moved, what's happening with the company, or your personal risk tolerance. Treat every badge as a prompt to go research further, not as a buy/sell instruction.

The top-line signal badge

Each card shows one big pill — Entry+, Entry, Watch, Exit, or Exit+. It's a single combined score built from all the smaller indicator badges below it, so you don't have to mentally combine eight numbers yourself.

BadgeMeaning
Entry+Strongest buy-side setup — most indicators agree, and the trend is strong.
EntryBuy-side setup — indicators lean bullish/oversold.
WatchMixed signals — no strong lean either way.
ExitSell-side setup — indicators lean bearish/overbought (for a position you already hold).
Exit+Strongest sell-side setup.

How the score is built — each factor below adds or subtracts points, then the total maps to one of the five tiers above:

FactorVote
Price below a recent low (7d/15d/30d/90d/180d/365d)+1 to +3 (deeper/longer low = bigger vote)
Price above a recent high−1 to −3 (same scale, in reverse)
RSI ≤ 30 (oversold) / ≥ 70 (overbought)+1 / −1
Stochastic oversold / overbought+1 / −1
Price below lower Bollinger Band / above upper band+1 / −1
Trend + MACD + Supertrend direction (combined into one vote, see note)+1 / −1, ×2 if ADX shows a strong trend, ×0 if ADX shows a weak/no trend
Trend, MACD, and Supertrend are three different ways of asking the same question — "is this stock trending up or down?" — so they're bundled into a single vote instead of three, otherwise they'd just outvote the genuinely different indicators (RSI, Stochastic, Bollinger Bands). ADX then scales that one combined vote up when the trend is strong, or zeroes it out when there's no real trend to confirm.

Below the signal badge

Vol confirmed

Only appears on Entry/Entry+ cards. It means trading volume was at least 1.2× the normal average on this signal. In an informal backtest on the core watchlist, Entry signals with confirmed volume won noticeably more often (67% vs 55%) than those without — so it's a visual nudge that this particular Entry looks more trustworthy, not a separate scored factor.

Buy / Stop and Sell / Trail stop lines

On an Entry/Entry+ card: Buy ~$X is just the current price, and Stop ~$Y is price minus 2× ATR (Average True Range) — a volatility-scaled reference for where a stop-loss might sit. It's shown for reference only; the page doesn't enforce it as a rule (testing showed a hard stop at that exact distance actually hurt returns — the Exit signal is meant to do that job instead).

On an Exit/Exit+ card: Sell ~$X is the current price, and Trail stop ~$Y is the current Supertrend value — the same number shown in the Supertrend badge.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

A momentum gauge that compares the size of recent up-moves to recent down-moves (standard 14-day lookback) to answer: has this stock moved too far, too fast, in one direction?

ValueReadingBadge shown
0–30Oversold — selling has been unusually heavy; a bounce is more likely than usual. Good for a dip-buy thesis.Green, e.g. "RSI 22 oversold"
31–69Neutral — no strong momentum extreme in either direction. Nothing to read into it alone.Plain grey, e.g. "RSI 53"
70–100Overbought — buying has been unusually heavy; a pullback is more likely than usual. Caution on new entries.Amber, e.g. "RSI 78 overbought"
Example: AMD at RSI 65 is in the neutral zone — leaning warm but not a red flag by itself. MSFT at RSI 53 is squarely neutral. Neither tells you much on its own; it's one of several votes in the overall signal score.
Caveat: in a strong, sustained trend RSI can stay "overbought" or "oversold" for weeks — it's measuring momentum extremity, not predicting an immediate reversal.

Stochastic (%K)

Similar idea to RSI but measures where today's close sits within its recent high-low trading range, rather than the size of gains vs. losses. It reacts faster and swings more sharply than RSI.

ValueReadingBadge shown
0–20Oversold — closing near the bottom of its recent range.Green, e.g. "Stoch 11 oversold"
21–79NeutralPlain grey, e.g. "Stoch 53"
80–100Overbought — closing near the top of its recent range.Amber, e.g. "Stoch 92 overbought"
Example: AMD's "Stoch 92 overbought" means it closed right at the top of its recent range — consistent with the "Above 30d high" badge on the same card. CRM's "Stoch 21" is just inside neutral, bordering oversold.

Uptrend / Downtrend

A simple directional read of the short/medium-term trend (no numeric value shown — just the current direction). There's no "bad" or "good" value here, only a binary state:

StateReading
Uptrend (green)Favorable backdrop for buy-side setups — price direction is currently working in an Entry's favor.
Downtrend (red)Favorable backdrop for sell-side setups, or a reason for caution on a new long entry.
On its own this just describes what price has been doing recently — it says nothing about how strong or reliable that trend is. Pair it with the ADX badge before trusting it.

Volume multiple (X.Xx volume)

Today's trading volume divided by its normal recent average. 1.0x would mean exactly average volume; the badge only appears once volume is unusual.

ValueReadingBadge shown
< 1.5×Nothing notable — ordinary volume, no real conviction signal either way.No badge shown
1.5× – 2.9×Elevated — meaningfully more participation than usual behind the move.Blue, e.g. "1.8x volume"
3×+Very heavy — a large, likely institutional or news-driven move.Blue, e.g. "4.3x volume"
Related but separate: the green "Vol confirmed" pill on an Entry/Entry+ card triggers at a lower bar (≥1.2×) — it's a different threshold used specifically to flag Entry signals worth a bit more trust, not this badge.

MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)

Compares a fast moving average to a slower one to gauge whether momentum is accelerating up or down. Only shown as a bullish/bearish read here, not a raw number.

StateReading
Bullish (green)Short-term momentum is running above longer-term momentum — supportive of an uptrend continuing.
Bearish (red)Short-term momentum is running below longer-term momentum — supportive of a downtrend continuing.
MACD is a lagging indicator — it confirms a trend that's already underway rather than predicting a turn. Don't treat a MACD flip alone as a timing trigger.

ADX (Average Directional Index)

Measures how strong the current trend is — regardless of direction. This is the badge that tells you how much weight to put on the Uptrend/Downtrend, MACD, and Supertrend badges.

ValueReadingBadge shown
0–19Weak/no trend — price is likely chopping sideways. Trend-direction badges are unreliable here (the scoring model discounts them to zero in this range).Grey, e.g. "Weak trend (ADX 15)"
20–25Developing — a trend may be forming but isn't confirmed yet.No badge shown
26+Strong trend — the directional badges are more trustworthy here (the scoring model doubles their weight).Purple, e.g. "Strong trend (ADX 32)"
Example: MSFT's "Strong trend (ADX 32)" alongside "Uptrend" and "MACD bearish" is a case worth noticing — a strong trend but with disagreeing signals underneath it is worth a closer look, not a snap read.

Supertrend

A volatility-based (ATR) trailing indicator that flips between a bullish and bearish state and prints a price level — that level acts as a de facto trailing stop reference.

StateReading
▲ green, bullishTrend is up; the dollar value shown is the price at which it would flip bearish if breached to the downside.
▼ red, bearishTrend is down; the dollar value shown is the price at which it would flip bullish if breached to the upside.
This is also where the "Trail stop" reference on Exit/Exit+ cards comes from — it's literally the same number as this badge.

Bollinger Bands (Above upper band / Below lower band)

Bands plotted a set statistical distance above and below a moving average, marking the range price "normally" trades within. A breach means price has moved outside that normal statistical range.

StateReadingBadge shown
Inside the bandsNothing notable — price is within its typical range.No badge shown
Below lower bandStretched low — statistically oversold, a contrarian-bullish read similar to low RSI.Green, "Below lower band"
Above upper bandStretched high — statistically overbought, a contrarian-bearish read similar to high RSI.Amber, "Above upper band"
Example: AMD showing "Above upper band" together with "Stoch 92 overbought" and "Above 30d high" is three different lenses agreeing the stock is statistically stretched to the upside right now.

ETF context (Sticker Search only)

On Sticker Search, looking up a known broad-market or sector ETF (VOO, QQQM, SPY, XLK, etc.) shows two extra stats instead of the fundamentals row above — a company-earnings valuation doesn't really apply to a fund, so ETFs get a more standard "timing an index" read instead.

StatWhat it isHow to read it
52-week highHow far the current price is below its trailing 365-day highBigger % = a deeper dip. This is the classic "how big is this discount" framing long-term index investors use, instead of short-term oversold bounces.
200-day trendPrice vs. its 200-day moving average, plus the % distanceAbove (green) = still in a long-term uptrend — a dip here is more likely a genuine discount. Below (red) = the long-term trend itself has turned down — many buy-the-dip strategies treat this as a reason to wait rather than a bigger discount.
Example read: "12% below 52-week high" + "Above 200-day trend" reads as a real pullback within an intact uptrend — the combination most buy-the-dip index strategies look for. The same 12% dip while "Below 200-day trend" is a weaker setup — the long-term trend has already broken down.

Sticker price row (fundamentals)

This only appears after you click Update Fundamentals on the main page (it's not fetched automatically, since fundamentals change quarterly, not daily). It's a rough intrinsic-value estimate, in the style of Phil Town's "Rule #1" sticker price method:

FieldMeaning
Sticker $Estimated fair-value price, projected from the growth rate shown
MOS $ (Margin of Safety)A discounted price below the sticker price — buying at or below this leaves room for estimation error
Growth %The assumed future growth rate used to project the sticker price
Bargain / Fair value / Above stickerWhere the current price sits relative to those two lines
Change the growth-rate assumption and the sticker price moves a lot — a "Bargain" label is only as good as that assumption. Worth sanity-checking against your own view of the company, not taking at face value.

The colored left border & corner badge

Separate from the signal badge — this just flags when price has broken a recent low or high outright:

  • Amber→red (below 7d up to below 365d low) = price fell under a recent floor. Darker/redder = a longer, more significant floor broken.
  • Light→dark blue (above 7d up to above 365d high) = price broke out above a recent ceiling.
  • "No new low/high" = price is within its recent range, nothing broken either way.

This is the same legend shown at the top of the main watchlist page.

The 7d/15d/30d/90d/180d/365d grid

For each lookback window, L is the lowest close and H is the highest close in that period. A bolded/colored L means price is currently below that window's low; a bolded/colored H means it's currently above that window's high. Use it to see at a glance how stretched the current price is versus its own recent history.

News

The most recent headline the Worker found for that ticker, linked to its source. Not curated or scored — just the latest item.

Chart (top tickers only)
