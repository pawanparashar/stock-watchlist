What do the colors mean? (the simple version)
BUY (green) All the clues agree this stock looks like a good one to look at buying. Like a traffic light turning green — it doesn't drive the car for you, but it says "go ahead and look closer."
SELL (red) All the clues agree this looks like a good time to think about selling, if you already own it. Like a red light — a signal to stop and check whether you should get out.
SIGNALS DISAGREEING (yellow) The main grade says one thing, but one of the other clues says the opposite — like your two best friends telling you two different things about the same movie. Don't trust it blindly either way. Read the badges on the card to see exactly what disagrees, and why, before deciding anything.
NEUTRAL (gray) Nothing exciting is happening. Not a good time to buy, not a good time to sell — just a "nothing to see here, keep watching" stock.
How to actually use this page:
  1. Look at the color strip on top of each card first — that's your starting point, not your answer.
  2. Green or red? Read the rest of that card (the badges, the chart, any news) to understand why it got that color.
  3. Yellow? Read even more carefully — something doesn't fully agree, so figure out which clue is the odd one out before doing anything.
  4. Gray? Nothing to do — just glance at it again next time you check.
  5. No matter the color: this page is a place to start looking, not a machine that tells you what to do. A real decision — especially with real money — should always get a second, careful look from you (and a trusted adult/advisor for bigger amounts).
